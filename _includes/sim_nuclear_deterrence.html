Analysis:

Mutual Assured Destruction (MAD) requires both sides to maintain credible second-strike capability — the ability to absorb a first strike and still inflict unacceptable damage. Paradoxically:
• Too few warheads invite a disarming first strike (instability)
• Too many warheads waste resources without improving security (overkill)
• Missile defense can be destabilizing: if one side believes it can intercept a retaliatory strike, it may be emboldened to strike first
• Launch-on-Warning reduces response time but increases the risk of accidental nuclear war from false alarms (cf. 1983 Soviet satellite incident, Stanislav Petrov)

The stable equilibrium requires both sides to be vulnerable to retaliation — a grim logic that kept the Cold War cold.
