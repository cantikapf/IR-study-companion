Lab 06 CASE FILE · DIPLOMATIC STRATEGY

Two-Table Pressure: Putnam's Ratification Maze

≈ 3 min 6 negotiation turns 2 AP twin-track budget Foundation level

Situation

International diplomacy is never played on a single board. Under Robert Putnam's Two-Level Game model, a state leader must negotiate simultaneously with foreign counterparts at Level I (international table) and with domestic legislators, party factions, and lobbies at Level II (domestic ratification).

Your Role

You are the Chief Diplomatic Envoy negotiating a high-stakes bilateral climate and trade accord over 6 turns. Each turn, you hold 2 Action Points (AP) to distribute across international bargaining and domestic constituency briefings.

Objectives

  • ★★★ Master Negotiator: Ratify treaty with Zone of Possible Agreement (ZOPA) ≥ 70%.
  • ★★ Seasoned Diplomat: Ratify treaty with ZOPA between 45% and 69%.
  • 0–1★ Involuntary Defection: Ratification defeated in domestic parliament or foreign walkout (ZOPA < 45%).

How It Works

  • Twin-Track Skew Constraint: The Win-Set expands only when both tracks advance in balance (max 1-point skew). If Level I outpaces Level II, backbenchers revolt; if Level II outpaces Level I, foreign envoys walk out.
  • 6 Resource Turns: Allocate [2 Int / 0 Dom], [1 Int / 1 Dom], or [0 Int / 2 Dom] each turn.
  • Turn 4 SIGINT: Intercept overnight congressional poll data.
