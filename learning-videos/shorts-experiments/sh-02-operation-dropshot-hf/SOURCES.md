## 3. Unverified Claims Dropped

To maintain zero-hallucination compliance with project editorial standards:

1. **"Soviet ICBM Corridor" Myth (Roadmap catalog row claim):**  
   The catalog synopsis in `SHORTS_CATALOG_WORKFLOW.md` referenced a *"Soviet ICBM corridor (1949)"*. This is technologically impossible and historically false: the Soviet Union's first intercontinental ballistic missile (the R-7 Semyorka) was not successfully flight-tested until August 1957 and did not achieve operational deployment until 1959. In 1949, both powers relied exclusively on long-range strategic bombers. Dropped completely from narration, headlines, chips, and dockets; replaced with the verified historical delivery mechanism: Strategic Air Command heavy bombers (B-36 Peacemaker and B-50) over Arctic polar routes.

2. **"Dropshot as an Approved Presidential Attack Directive":**  
   Popular Cold War commentary sometimes frames Operation Dropshot as an approved White House directive to launch World War III. Dropshot was an unapproved contingency study developed by the Joint Chiefs of Staff's planning committee. The Department of Defense never authorized it for execution, and it was withdrawn in February 1951. Stated accurately in Beat 5.

3. **"300 Atomic Bombs Available in 1949":**  
   Conflating Dropshot's 1957 projected requirement of 300 atomic bombs with America's actual 1949 stockpile. The US arsenal in 1949 held approximately 170 nuclear warheads. This disparity forms the verified core paradox of Beat 6.
