    Defensive vs. Offensive Realism: Anarchy, Power, and the Security Dilemma

    While both neorealist branches accept that the international system is anarchic, they fundamentally diverge on a critical question: how much power does a state need to guarantee its survival?

    Learning Outcomes

    • Distinguish Kenneth Waltz's defensive structural realism from John Mearsheimer's offensive structural realism.
    • Analyze how the security dilemma creates unintended spirals of tension even between security-seeking states.
    • Evaluate historical and contemporary Great Power rivalries through the lens of power maximization vs. status quo balance.

    1. The Anarchic Imperative: Survival as Primary Motive

    In international relations theory, structural realism (neorealism) treats states as rational unitary actors operating under structural anarchy—the absence of a centralized global sovereign capable of enforcing contracts or preventing aggression. Because there is no 911 in global politics, states must rely on self-help.

    However, the theoretical consensus fractures over the strategic behavior anarchy incentivizes:

    Core Analytical Division: Are states structurally motivated to be security maximizers seeking an appropriate amount of power to survive (Defensive Realism), or are they compelled to be power maximizers striving for regional hegemony because absolute security can never be assured (Offensive Realism)?

    2. Defensive Realism: Balance over Hegemony

    Pioneered by Kenneth Waltz (Theory of International Politics, 1979) and expanded by Robert Jervis and Stephen Van Evera, defensive realism posits that the international system provides strong incentives for states to maintain their position within the balance of power, rather than pursuing expansion.

    Defensive realists argue that expansionist policies are inherently self-defeating because they prompt the formation of counter-balancing coalitions. When Napoleonic France or Imperial Germany attempted to dominate the European continent, surrounding powers banded together to restore structural equilibrium.
